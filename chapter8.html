8. Information security and cyber law

Dear NEB Class 11 Computer Science students, welcome to Chapter 9: Information Security and Cyber Law! In today's rapidly evolving digital world, understanding how to protect information and navigate online legalities is not just important for your personal safety but also a crucial part of your Computer Science curriculum. The Curriculum Development Centre (CDC) and National Examinations Board (NEB) emphasize these topics, and you can expect questions on them in your board examinations, particularly in the Short Answer and Long Answer sections. This chapter will equip you with the knowledge to understand digital ethics, identify cyber threats, and comprehend the legal frameworks, including Nepal's own Cyber Law and ICT Policy, that govern our digital society. Digital Society and Computer Ethics A digital society is one where advanced technologies, especially information and communication technologies (ICTs), are deeply integrated into daily life and culture, facilitating communication, learning, and commerce. This integration brings numerous benefits like social connectivity, communication speed, versatile working, and learning opportunities. However, it also introduces ethical dilemmas that require careful consideration, which is where computer ethics comes in. Computer Ethics refers to a set of moral principles and guidelines that govern the responsible use of computers and information technology. These ethics help individuals, organizations, and governments make intelligent decisions about actions involving computers, ensuring they are morally right and do not harm others or society. Ethical Issues in Computing: Privacy: Respecting the privacy of others means not accessing or sharing personal information without consent. Unauthorized access to data can lead to a loss of privacy. Intellectual Property: This involves respecting copyrights, licenses, and patents of software and digital content. Illegal copying, distribution, or use of software (software piracy) is a significant ethical concern. Accuracy: Ensuring the accuracy of information processed and stored by computers. Fabricating evidence using a computer is unethical. Access Rights: Preventing unauthorized access to computer systems and networks. This includes hacking, which is gaining unauthorized access to a system. Harmful Actions: Avoiding the use of computers to hurt others, obstruct their work, or spread misinformation. Digital Divide: The gap between those who have access to information technology and those who do not. Addressing this divide is an ethical responsibility for governments and societies. Concept of Information Security Information security, often abbreviated as "infosec," is the practice of protecting information and information systems from unauthorized access, use, disclosure, disruption, modification, or destruction. Its primary goal is to ensure the **Confidentiality, Integrity, and Availability (CIA)** of data. Confidentiality: Ensuring that information is accessible only to those authorized to have access. Integrity: Maintaining the accuracy and completeness of information and preventing unauthorized modification. Availability: Ensuring that authorized users have timely and reliable access to information. Threats to Information Security: A security threat is a potential danger that could exploit vulnerabilities in a computer system and cause harm. Threats can be intentional or accidental. Common threats include: Interception: Unauthorized access to or stealing of information. Interruption: Making services or data unavailable to legitimate users. Modification: Unauthorized alteration of data or tampering with services. Fabrication: Introducing false data into a system. Malicious Software (Malware): Programs designed to harm computer systems (discussed in detail later). Hacking: Gaining unauthorized access to computer systems or networks. Phishing: Deceptive attempts to obtain sensitive information (discussed in detail later). Denial of Service (DoS) Attacks: Overwhelming a system with traffic to make it unavailable. Encryption and Decryption: Cryptography is the art and science of protecting information and communications through the use of codes, ensuring that only those for whom the information is intended can read and process it. It is also referred to as encryption and decryption. Encryption: The process of converting readable plaintext data into unreadable, scrambled data called ciphertext, using an algorithm and a key. This protects data when it's stored or transmitted across networks. Decryption: The reverse process of converting ciphertext back into readable plaintext using a key. Types of Encryption: Symmetric Key Cryptography (Private Key Cryptography): Uses a single, secret key for both encryption and decryption. Both the sender and receiver must possess this same secret key. Faster encryption and decryption process. Reliable for fewer users or communications. Asymmetric Key Cryptography (Public Key Cryptography): Uses two different keys: a public key for encryption and a private key for decryption. The public key is freely distributed, while the private key remains secret with the recipient. Slower encryption and decryption process compared to symmetric key. Can be used for digital signatures and authentication systems, making it reliable for a large number of users. Concept of Cybercrime Cybercrime refers to any criminal activity that involves the use of computers or the internet, either as a tool to commit the crime or as the target of the crime. Cybercriminals exploit computer technology to gain unauthorized access, steal information, disrupt operations, or cause harm. Types of Cybercrimes: Hacking: Gaining unauthorized access to a computer system or network, often to steal data, manipulate information, or disrupt services. Phishing: A social engineering attack where fraudsters disguise themselves as trustworthy entities (e.g., banks, government agencies) in electronic communications (emails, messages) to trick victims into revealing sensitive information like usernames, passwords, or credit card details. Identity Theft: Stealing and using another person's personal information (e.g., credit card numbers, Social Security numbers) without their permission to commit fraud or other crimes. Malware Attacks: Involving malicious software like viruses, worms, Trojans, ransomware, and spyware designed to infiltrate or damage a device. Cyberbullying/Cyberstalking: Harassing, intimidating, threatening, or humiliating individuals online, often through social media or messaging platforms. Software Piracy: The illegal copying, distribution, or use of copyrighted software in violation of its license terms. Online Fraud: Deceptive practices carried out over the internet to trick individuals into financial loss, such as online transaction fraud or fake schemes. Ransomware: A type of malware that encrypts a victim's data, making it inaccessible until a ransom is paid to the attacker. Implications of Cybercrime: Cybercrime has severe consequences for individuals, businesses, and governments: Financial Loss: Direct monetary loss from fraud, theft, or ransom payments. Data Breach and Loss: Sensitive personal or organizational data can be stolen, altered, or destroyed. Loss of Privacy: Personal information can be exposed, leading to identity theft and harassment. Reputational Damage: Businesses and individuals can suffer significant damage to their reputation. System Downtime: Cyberattacks can disrupt computer operations and services, leading to productivity loss. Legal Consequences: Perpetrators face legal penalties, including fines and imprisonment, under cyber laws. Malicious Software and Spam Malicious Software (Malware): Malware is a general term for any software program specifically designed to disrupt computer operation, gather sensitive information, or gain unauthorized access to computer systems. Computer viruses are a type of malware. Other common types include: Viruses: Programs that attach themselves to legitimate software and spread to other computers when the infected program is executed, often causing damage to data or system functionality. Worms: Standalone malicious programs that replicate themselves and spread across networks without needing a host program, often consuming network bandwidth. Trojan Horses: Malicious programs disguised as legitimate software. Users are tricked into installing them, after which they can create backdoors for attackers, steal data, or cause other harm. Spyware: Programs that secretly monitor a user's computer activity, collecting sensitive information like passwords, browsing history, and bank account details, and sending it to unauthorized third parties. Rootkits: Collections of software tools that enable unauthorized control over a computer system at the "root" or administrator level, often hiding their presence from detection. Ransomware: Malware that encrypts files on a computer and demands a ransom payment (usually in cryptocurrency) for their decryption. Phishing: As discussed, phishing is a deceptive technique used to acquire sensitive information by masquerading as a trustworthy entity in an electronic communication. Phishing emails often use generic greetings, create a sense of urgency, and contain suspicious links or attachments. Spam: Spam refers to unwanted and unsolicited messages, typically sent in bulk to a large number of recipients, often for advertising or malicious purposes. While not all spam is malware, it can contain malicious links or attachments that lead to malware infections. Preventive Measures against Malicious Software and Spam: Use Antivirus Software: Install and regularly update reputable antivirus and anti-malware software to detect, block, and remove threats. Be Cautious with Emails: Do not open spam emails or click on suspicious links or attachments from unknown sources. Enable email filters to divert potential phishing emails. Avoid Pirated Software: Do not use pirated software, as it often contains malware. Scan Removable Devices: Always scan USB drives, memory cards, and other removable storage devices before use. Use Strong Passwords and MFA: Employ strong, unique passwords and enable multi-factor authentication (MFA) for online accounts. Keep Software Updated: Regularly update operating systems, browsers, and applications to patch security vulnerabilities. Cyber Awareness Training: Educate yourself and others on how to identify and report suspicious emails and online threats. Protection from Cybercrime Effective protection from cybercrime involves a multi-layered approach combining technology, policies, and user awareness. Cybersecurity Measures: Access Management: Control who has access to data and systems. Implement strong login controls and give users only the necessary privileges (least privilege). Security Monitoring: Continuously monitor systems and networks for suspicious activities and intrusions. Data Encryption: Encrypt sensitive data both at rest (stored) and in transit (transmitted) to protect its confidentiality. Regular Software Updates: Keep all software, including operating systems, applications, and security tools, up-to-date to patch known vulnerabilities. User Education: Train users on cybersecurity best practices, including recognizing phishing attempts and safe browsing habits. Firewall: A firewall acts as a digital barrier or gatekeeper between a computer network and external networks (like the internet). It monitors and controls incoming and outgoing network traffic based on predefined security rules. Its main job is to block unauthorized access, prevent intrusions, and filter out harmful traffic, thus protecting devices from viruses and malware. Firewalls can be hardware-based (in routers) or software-based. Antivirus Software: Antivirus software is a program designed to detect, prevent, and remove malicious software, including viruses, worms, Trojans, spyware, and ransomware, from computer systems. It continuously monitors the system, scans files, and compares them against a database of known threats (signature-based detection) or uses behavioral analysis to identify new threats. Data Backup: Data backup is the process of creating secure copies of important data and storing them in separate, secure locations. This is a crucial measure for disaster recovery, allowing you to restore information in case of hardware failure, accidental deletion, cyberattacks (like ransomware), or other data loss incidents. Best practices include regular, automatic backups, storing copies off-site or in the cloud, and periodically testing data restoration. Intellectual Property Right (IPR) Intellectual Property (IP) refers to creations of the mind, such as inventions, literary and artistic works, designs, and symbols, names, and images used in commerce. In the digital age, protecting these creations is vital. Types of Intellectual Property Rights: Copyright: A legal right that grants the creator of original literary, dramatic, musical, and artistic works (including software code, books, music, movies, and websites) exclusive rights to determine how their work is used and distributed. It protects the expression of an idea, not the idea itself. Patents: A legal right granted to an inventor for a new, useful, and non-obvious invention (e.g., a new process, machine, manufacture, or composition of matter). A patent gives the inventor the right to prevent others from making, using, or selling the invention without permission. In software, patents can protect algorithms or specific functions. Trademarks: A distinctive sign, design, or expression that identifies products or services of a particular source from those of others. Examples include brand names, logos, and slogans. Trademarks help consumers identify goods and services and prevent confusion in the marketplace. Importance in Digital Content: IPR is crucial for digital content because it: Protects Creators: Ensures that creators are recognized and rewarded for their original digital works, encouraging innovation. Prevents Piracy: Helps combat illegal copying, distribution, and use of software, music, videos, and other digital content. Fosters Innovation: By granting exclusive rights for a period, IPR incentivizes investment in research and development in the digital sector. Builds Trust: For businesses, trademarks build brand recognition and trust with consumers in the digital marketplace. ICT Policy in Nepal Nepal's Information and Communication Technology (ICT) Policy aims to transform the nation into an information and knowledge-based society and economy. The policy recognizes ICT as a key enabler for sustainable development, poverty reduction, and good governance. Key aspects and objectives of Nepal's ICT Policy (e.g., National Information and Communication Technology Policy, 2015) include: Vision: To transform Nepal into an information and knowledge-based society and economy. Mission: To create conditions for the intensified development and growth of the ICT sector as a key driver for Nepal's sustainable development and poverty reduction strategies. Digital Literacy: Aiming for a high percentage of digital literacy among the population. Broadband Access: Expanding universal broadband access across the country, including rural areas. E-Governance: Promoting the use of ICT in all government activities to enhance transparency, efficiency, and citizen participation in service delivery. ICT Infrastructure Development: Strengthening telecommunications infrastructure, including fiber optic networks. IT Human Resource Development: Strengthening ICT education at all levels, establishing Centers of Excellence, and promoting digital skills. Cyber Security: Developing and implementing cybersecurity policies to enhance individual and collective security while preserving privacy rights. E-Commerce Promotion: Facilitating the growth of e-commerce and Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs) through ICT. Research and Development: Promoting ICT research and development. The policy emphasizes government leadership in facilitating investment and resources, with private-sector implementation, and aims to mainstream ICT use across all sectors of the Nepalese economy. Concept of Cyber Law in Nepal Cyber Law is the legal framework that governs activities in cyberspace, encompassing computers, computer networks, the internet, data, and software. It addresses the legal issues arising from the use of information technology, particularly concerning the internet. In Nepal, the primary cyber law is the Electronic Transaction Act (ETA) of 2063 BS (2008 AD). This act was formulated to legalize various trading activities through global computer networks, boost e-governance, and provide a legal basis for electronic and digital signatures. Key aspects and implications of Cyber Law in Nepal (Electronic Transaction Act, 2063 BS): Legalization of Electronic Transactions: Provides legal recognition to electronic records and digital signatures, making online transactions legally valid. Provisions Against Cybercrimes: Contains strong provisions for punishment against various cybercrimes. Specific cybercrimes mentioned include: Hacking into computer systems. Damage to computer source code. Breach of privacy. Faking digital signatures. Publishing illegal materials in electronic form. Penalties: The Act specifies penalties, including imprisonment and fines, depending on the nature of the crime. For example, unauthorized access to any computer system can result in imprisonment for up to three years, a fine of up to two hundred thousand rupees, or both. Establishment of Judicial Bodies: It established specialized judicial bodies like the IT Tribunal and Appellate Tribunal to handle cases related to computer and cybercrimes. Controller of Certifying Authorities: The government appoints a controller for public key certifying authorities to regulate digital signatures. Areas Covered: Cyber law in Nepal deals with electronic and digital signatures, computer crime, intellectual property in the digital realm, data protection and privacy, and telecommunication laws. The implementation of cyber law faces challenges such as a lack of awareness among the general public and law enforcement agencies, and the need for continuous updates to keep pace with evolving technology and cyber threats. Implication: Victims can suffer financial loss, identity theft, and compromise of their online accounts. Hacking: This is gaining unauthorized access to a computer system or network. Hackers may steal data, disrupt operations, or install malicious software. Malware Attacks (e.g., Ransomware): Malicious software like ransomware encrypts a victim's data, making it inaccessible until a ransom is paid. Implication: Individuals face significant data loss, financial extortion, and disruption of their computer usage if they cannot recover their files. What is information security? Explain the roles of Firewall, Antivirus software, and Data Backup in protecting a computer system from cyber threats. (8 marks) Solution: Information security is the practice of protecting information and information systems from unauthorized access, use, disclosure, disruption, modification, or destruction, ensuring confidentiality, integrity, and availability of data. The roles of Firewall, Antivirus software, and Data Backup in protection: Firewall: A firewall acts as a security barrier between a computer network and external networks (like the internet). It monitors and controls incoming and outgoing network traffic based on predefined security rules, blocking unauthorized access and filtering out harmful data. This prevents external threats from reaching internal systems. Antivirus Software: This software detects, prevents, and removes malicious programs such as viruses, worms, and Trojans from a computer system. It continuously scans files and system activity, comparing them against known threat signatures and using behavioral analysis to identify and neutralize malware. Data Backup: Data backup involves creating secure copies of important data and storing them in separate, safe locations. In the event of data loss due to hardware failure, accidental deletion, or cyberattacks (like ransomware), backups allow for the recovery of information, ensuring operational continuity and minimizing the impact of data loss.
